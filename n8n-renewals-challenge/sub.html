How the CRM supports this (my standard setup)

  • Renewal Status picklist (Not started → Engaging customer → Negotiation → Quoted → Waiting for PO → PO received, plus Cancelled). I’d add Closed won, and give each status written exit criteria so the reps all use it the same way.
  • Probability = status × countdown. A later status closer to the date means a higher probability. An early status close to the date means a lower one, because the cycle can’t finish in time. That is the signal managers usually miss. The value is multiplied by CSM health. Auto-renewals with no blocker get an 80% floor.
  • Product lines on each renewal opportunity are tagged Renewal / Upsell / Downgrade / Cross-sell / Churn. Original ARR is kept for comparison. New ARR adds the 5% uplift to the renewed base, prices any change at list, then applies the discount. The difference shows green or red. An approval column reads policy C (Finance or VP). The opportunity roll-up calculates churn, contraction, upsell, discount and new ARR automatically, so the forecast never relies on someone retyping a number.
  • Indexation: the default is a 5% uplift on every 1-year renewal line. On 2-year deals, the uplift can be waived within the multi-year 5% allowance with no approval.

Could we set up 30 minutes with you and Priya on Thursday or Friday? It would help if I came in knowing:

  2. who signs, and by when, and

Best, Paula Renewals, n8n

Why this email: it acknowledges the support problem first, and it says no to the four asks once, clearly, without arguing each one in writing. It points towards value and trade-offs rather than a price, and it moves the negotiation onto a call with the decision makers.

B. One-page negotiation plan

The numbers (current price €36,000; 1-year list renewal with 5% uplift €37,800). Discount is measured against €37,800, because policy treats a waived uplift as a discount.

What they asked for What it costs n8n Who must approve
25% discount €27,000 (28.6% off list) €10.8k a year, and a precedent visible across 220 other renewals VP CS (above 12%)

What’s really going on: a budget cut (real, according to the CSM), a 10% usage dip (small), and a new procurement lead who needs a visible win. Sam values the product. The two slow support escalations are the emotional lever behind “evaluate alternatives”. For n8n, the realistic alternative is self-hosting the free Community edition. Moving off Enterprise means giving up SSO/SAML, RBAC and projects, environments and Git source control, log streaming, external secrets, and SLA-backed support. They’d also take on hosting and DevOps work, which at 0.2 FTE is more than the discount they’re asking for.

Target outcome (no approvals needed):

  • 2-year term at €36,000 a year, flat. The uplift is waived, which is 4.8% off list and inside the 5% multi-year allowance. Contract value €72k, and their budget is protected for 2026–2027.
  • Net 45 (allowed), auto-renewal kept, no termination right.
  • A support remediation plan: a written review of both escalations, a named escalation path, and a quarterly support review with the CSM.
  • Signed by 30 November.

Walk-away position:

  • Never: termination for convenience, removing auto-renewal, or more than 12% off the current plan. I won’t take any of these to approval myself.
  • Price floor on the current plan: €33,264 a year on a 2-year term (option C). On a 1-year term, €36,000 flat with Finance approval.
  • If they need less than that, the answer is right-sizing at list price (D), not a deeper discount. I’d rather book €7k of honest contraction than a 25% discount that resets the price.
  • Escalate to VP only if Brightloop brings something new: a 3-year term, or a strategic logo or case study. Even then, my recommendation would be no more than about 15%.

What I’d give, and what I ask for each time (“if… then…”):

  • Uplift waiver → in exchange for a 2-year term
  • Net 60 → in exchange for a 2-year term and signature by 30 Nov
  • A deeper discount (up to 12%) → in exchange for a 2-year term, payment upfront, a reference, and the deadline
  • Concern about auto-renewal → offer a renewal reminder from us 90 days ahead and keep the standard notice period. This gives them control without removing the clause. Any change to the notice period goes to Legal.
  • Wanting an exit right → the 2-year price lock is the protection they actually need. The fear is being locked into a price they can’t afford, not being locked into a product they use.
  • Free: the support review, an exec sponsor call, and a usage optimisation session from the CSM

Who I involve:

  • Finance: pre-approve up to 12% on a 2-year term, net 60 and the ramp, before the call.
  • Legal: confirm the wording for declining the termination right and keeping auto-renewal, and sign off any notice-period tweak.
  • VP CS: a heads-up that this is €36k at risk. No approval is needed unless the discount goes above 12%.
  • CSM: owns Sam and the success plan, and brings the usage data.
  • Support lead: a root-cause review of the two escalations by 30 Oct.
  • My manager: a weekly status update.

Part 3: Scaling the renewal motion

The step I automate: daily renewal triage, forecast scoring and escalation

Why this step: there are 221 Q4 renewals and four overwhelmed CSMs. The failure in this book is timing, not negotiation skill. Quotes go out late (Northwind has none at 9 days out), auto-renewals fail silently (Pixelforge’s expired card), notice dates pass unnoticed (Halden, Pixelforge, Lumen), and the forecast is a guess. Triage, the first thing I did by hand in Part 1, is the job a machine should do every morning. I built a working prototype in n8n.

Workflow: Renewal forecast and escalation engine (in my n8n Cloud: https://paulazabala95.app.n8n.cloud/workflow/mvDDTTwQYTpurdtP). The source is in n8n/. It is built on my existing Daily renewal alerts workflow, which drafts customer emails with AI and sends them only after a CSM approves.

Weekdays 7:30 ─► Read renewal pipeline (CRM / Sheet)
                 └► Score renewals + raise alerts (Code)
                     ├► Write scores back to the pipeline (CRM hygiene)
                     ├► Build daily brief ─► Email to Renewals Specialist
                     └► P1? ─► Escalation email to owner + manager
Trigger Schedule, weekdays at 7:30. In production, also a CRM trigger when Renewal Status, Renewal Date or Amount changes.
Systems Prototype: Google Sheets (pipeline) and Gmail. Production: Salesforce (renewal opportunities and product lines), Stripe/billing (card status, invoice entity), the product usage warehouse (90-day execution trend), Slack (#renewals-alerts, DMs to owners), and e-signature status.
What it calculates Days to renewal, 30/60/90 window, month and quarter, the status × countdown probability (the same matrix as the workbook) × health, and weighted new ARR including the 5% uplift.
Alerts OVERDUE · QUOTE_LEAD_TIME (manual, ≤60 days, no quote) · SIGNATURE_RISK (≤14 days, not with procurement) · PAYMENT_BLOCKER (auto-renewal with an expired card or wrong entity) · NOTICE_WINDOW (date to send the uplift notice or watch for cancellation) · HEALTH_RISK · UPSELL_SIGNAL · BLOCKER_OPEN · CRM_HYGIENE (no status, owner or ARR)
Output One brief per day grouped by quarter and month, with actions sorted P1 → P3. One escalation email per P1 account. The calculated fields are written back to the pipeline so the forecast report is always current.

Tested on the 10 accounts in this book (pinned data, so no emails were sent). It raised 5 P1s (Vantage, Northwind, Pixelforge, Halden, Tessera) and 5 P2s. That matches my manual triage in Part 1, which took me about an hour. The workflow takes seconds and runs every day.

What a human still checks

  • The forecast call. The workflow gives a system-weighted number. I make the commit / late / lost call and record it in the CRM. When the two differ by more than 10%, I review that deal.
  • Every P1: I confirm an owner and a date by 10:00.
  • Anything going to a customer: AI drafts the emails, and a person approves before sending. This is the approval step in my existing workflow.
  • Commercial terms: discounts, approvals and contract changes are never automated.
  • Recalibration: every quarter I compare the probability matrix with actual outcomes and adjust it.
