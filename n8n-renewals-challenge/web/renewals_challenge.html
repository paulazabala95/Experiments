Part 3

Get the approved quote out, signed and tracked

Once a quote is approved, the rest is repetition: email, order form, send, update the CRM, chase. That's the work that doesn't scale to 221 renewals a quarter.

My rule: the workflow never prices anything. The CRM and CPQ own the quote, the order form and every number; the workflow mirrors them. At my current company there was no CPQ, so I built an agent that ran our quote tool with deterministic pricing and a human approving every quote. Here I assume n8n has a CPQ.

Every day 8:00 ─► Renewals (unsigned, ≤90 days) ─┐ └► Approved quotes from CPQ ──────┴► Match quote to renewal ─► Route On track / Upsell ► AI drafts email (offer copied word for word) ► Renewals Manager approves ► send with order form ► Status = Quoted Chase signature ──► AI follow-up at day 3 and day 7 ─────────────► Renewals Manager approves ► send Risk ─────────────► alert to the CSM, no customer email Quote not ready ──► reminder to finish the quote in the CPQ

Built and tested

  • An extended copy of my existing n8n renewal-alerts workflow. The JSON is attached to the email and can be imported.
  • Prototype data: Google Sheet tabs standing in for Salesforce and the CPQ. In production, the order form goes out for e-signature.
  • Tested on five accounts, one per route, with no real emails sent.

Human in the loop, and how I'd know it works

  • A person approves every customer-facing email. Pricing and terms stay in the CPQ. At-risk accounts go to the CSM.
  • Quoted by day 60: 1 of 7 → 100% · approved quote to sent: same day · emails with a wrong number: 0 · AI drafts approved without edits, tracked weekly.
  • Lesson from testing: an early draft leaked internal notes. Autonomy is earned, not switched on from day one.
