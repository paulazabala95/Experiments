>

>
> 1. the budget figure you need to land on,
> 2. who signs, and by when, and

>

>

> Paula
> Renewals, n8n

*Why this email:* it acknowledges the support problem first, and it says no to the four asks once, clearly, without arguing each one in writing. It points towards value and trade-offs rather than a price, and it moves the negotiation onto a call with the decision makers.

## B. One-page negotiation plan

**The numbers** (current price €36,000; 1-year list renewal with 5% uplift **€37,800**). Discount is measured against €37,800, because policy treats a waived uplift as a discount.

| | What they asked for | What it costs n8n | Who must approve |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% discount | €27,000 (28.6% off list) | €10.8k a year, and a precedent visible across 220 other renewals | VP CS (above 12%) |
| Net 60 | | Low (cash timing) | Finance |
| Remove auto-renewal | | Turns a renewal into a new sale every year | Legal + VP CS |
| 3-month termination for convenience | | Turns annual ARR into quarterly ARR. It can't be forecast. | Legal, normally declined |
| Smaller plan | | Allowed at list price | None |

**What's really going on:** a budget cut (real, according to the CSM), a 10% usage dip (small), and a new procurement lead who needs a visible win. Sam values the product. The two slow support escalations are the emotional lever behind "evaluate alternatives". For n8n, the realistic alternative is **self-hosting the free Community edition**. Moving off Enterprise means giving up SSO/SAML, RBAC and projects, environments and Git source control, log streaming, external secrets, and SLA-backed support. They'd also take on hosting and DevOps work, which at 0.2 FTE is more than the discount they're asking for.

**Target outcome (no approvals needed):**

- **2-year term at €36,000 a year, flat.** The uplift is waived, which is 4.8% off list and inside the 5% multi-year allowance. Contract value €72k, and their budget is protected for 2026–2027.
- **Net 45** (allowed), auto-renewal kept, no termination right.
- A **support remediation plan**: a written review of both escalations, a named escalation path, and a quarterly support review with the CSM.
- Signed by **30 November**.

**Options I can trade within (Finance pre-approval secured before the call):**

| Option | Price per year | vs list | Approval | What I ask in return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Target | €36,000 × 2 years | −4.8% | None | Signed by 30 Nov |
| B. Ramp | Year 1 €32,400 / Year 2 €37,800 (average €35,100) | −7.1% | Finance | 2-year term, annual payment upfront |
| C. Floor, same plan | €33,264 × 2 years | −12% | Finance | 2-year term, net 60 only here, case study or reference, signed by 30 Nov |
| D. Right-size | Tier 2 at list (assumed €28,800) × 1 year | List | None | Usage review first. Upgrade path at pro-rata. |

**Walk-away position:**

- **Never:** termination for convenience, removing auto-renewal, or more than 12% off the current plan. I won't take any of these to approval myself.
- **Price floor on the current plan:** €33,264 a year on a 2-year term (option C). On a 1-year term, €36,000 flat with Finance approval.
- **If they need less than that,** the answer is **right-sizing at list price (D)**, not a deeper discount. I'd rather book €7k of honest contraction than a 25% discount that resets the price.
- **Escalate to VP only** if Brightloop brings something new: a 3-year term, or a strategic logo or case study. Even then, my recommendation would be no more than about 15%.

**What I'd give, and what I ask for each time ("if… then…"):**

- Uplift waiver → in exchange for a 2-year term
- Net 60 → in exchange for a 2-year term and signature by 30 Nov
- A deeper discount (up to 12%) → in exchange for a 2-year term, payment upfront, a reference, and the deadline
- Concern about auto-renewal → offer a **renewal reminder from us 90 days ahead** and keep the standard notice period. This gives them control without removing the clause. Any change to the notice period goes to Legal.
- Wanting an exit right → the **2-year price lock** is the protection they actually need. The fear is being locked into a price they can't afford, not being locked into a product they use.
- Free: the support review, an exec sponsor call, and a usage optimisation session from the CSM

**Who I involve:**

- **Finance:** pre-approve up to 12% on a 2-year term, net 60 and the ramp, before the call.
- **Legal:** confirm the wording for declining the termination right and keeping auto-renewal, and sign off any notice-period tweak.
- **VP CS:** a heads-up that this is €36k at risk. No approval is needed unless the discount goes above 12%.
- **CSM:** owns Sam and the success plan, and brings the usage data.
- **Support lead:** a root-cause review of the two escalations by 30 Oct.
- **My manager:** a weekly status update.

## C. Call playbook (15 minutes, I keep time)

| Min | Segment | What I do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Open | Agenda, time check, confirm who decides and who signs |
| 1–4 | Discovery (Sam) | Budget figure, dates, what "alternatives" means, what the 10% dip actually is (a seasonal workflow? a migration?) |
| 4–6 | Support | Own the two escalations, share the remediation plan, ask what "good" looks like |
| 6–10 | Options (Priya joins) | Show A and D side by side, anchor on A. Keep B and C in reserve. |
| 10–13 | Trade | Only "if… then…". One concession per thing I get back. Never split the difference. |
| 13–15 | Close | Summarise what we agreed, name an owner for each open item, set the date the order form goes out, confirm signature by 30 Nov |

### Surprises I expect, and how I'd answer

| They say | I answer |
|---|---|
| "We have a competitor quote 40% cheaper." | "Can you share what's in scope? Most quotes leave out migration, rebuilding the workflows, and Enterprise controls. Let's compare total cost of ownership." I don't match the price. I trade on term and right-sizing. |
| "We'll just self-host the free version." | That's a real option, and I respect it. I walk through what they'd give up (SSO, environments, Git, log streaming, SLA support) and what DevOps would cost them. I offer a technical session with Sam. |
| Priya: "Net 60 is company policy, non-negotiable." | "Understood. I can do net 60 on the 2-year structure." (Finance has pre-approved this.) |
| "All four points, or no deal." | I take it as an opening position. "I can't do the exit clause or remove auto-renewal for anyone. What problem are they solving for you?" Then I solve that problem: the price lock and the 90-day reminder. |
| "Fine, 3 years for 25% off." | "A 3-year deal changes the picture, and I'll take it to my VP. I'm not able to commit to 25%." I don't concede on the call. I come back within 48 hours. |
| Priya reveals a budget of €30k | Design to the budget. Ramp year 1 to about €32k, or right-size to Tier 2 plus an execution add-on. I show which fits their usage. |
| "Sam is leaving" / "Sam isn't the decision maker" | Multi-thread: ask for an intro to the budget owner, and get the CSM and an exec sponsor in front of the new person. |
| "We need it signed today, final offer." | I only offer what's already pre-approved (A–C). Anything else, I come back within 24 hours with an answer. |
| "Give us service credits for the escalations." | Credits are a discount by another name. I offer the remediation plan and quarterly support review, and point to the SLA. |
| "Remove the uplift on a 1-year renewal." | That's a discount under our policy. Two years flat gets you the same thing at no extra cost. |
| Silence or delay until 14 Dec | I set the date the order form goes out. If nothing is signed by 1 Dec, I escalate exec to exec. |

**Debrief line I'd use:** "I protected price integrity by moving the conversation from *discount* to *term, structure and right-size*, and every concession was traded."
